Bali Belly: Symptoms, How Long It Lasts, What to Eat, Cost and Insurance

By Dripwell Bali Medical Team • Medically reviewed by dr. Florensa Krismawati, S.Ked • Updated: November 2026 • 7 Min Read

Bali belly is travellers' diarrhoea caught in Bali, usually from bacteria in food or water. It causes diarrhoea and stomach cramps, most people recover in 3 to 5 days with fluids and rest, and the main danger is dehydration. This guide covers symptoms, how long it lasts, what to eat, when to go to hospital, treatment costs and travel insurance.

Bali belly symptoms

Symptoms usually start suddenly, often a few hours to a day after eating or drinking something contaminated. The most common are:

  • Loose or watery diarrhoea, often several times a day
  • Stomach cramps that come in waves
  • Nausea, and sometimes vomiting
  • Bloating, loss of appetite and tiredness
  • Sometimes a mild fever

Signs that you are becoming dehydrated: strong thirst, dry mouth, dark or very little urine, dizziness when you stand up and unusual weakness. Dehydration is the most common complication, so watch for it closely.

How long does Bali belly last?

Most cases improve on their own within 3 to 5 days. The first day is usually the worst, and many people feel clearly better by day two or three if they keep drinking.

See a doctor if your symptoms last more than 3 days, keep getting worse, or come with any of the warning signs in the hospital section below. Diarrhoea that continues for more than two weeks, during or after your trip, also needs a doctor's check and possibly stool tests.

What to eat and drink with Bali belly

Drinks come first. Replacing fluids and salts matters more than food in the first day.

  • Oral rehydration solution (ORS): the best drink for diarrhoea. In Bali it is sold as oralit at pharmacies (apotek) and minimarkets. Mix it with bottled water as the packet says.
  • Small, frequent sips: a few mouthfuls every few minutes is easier to keep down than a full glass at once.
  • Bottled or boiled water and clear soup also help. Sports drinks and fruit juice alone do not replace salts well.

When you feel ready to eat, start small with simple, bland food:

  • Plain white rice or rice porridge (bubur)
  • Bananas
  • Toast, plain crackers or plain bread
  • Clear chicken or vegetable soup
  • Boiled potatoes or steamed vegetables later on

Avoid until you have recovered: alcohol, coffee and energy drinks, dairy, spicy food, greasy or fried food, raw salads and ice from unknown sources. Our article on how to recover fast from Bali belly goes through recovery step by step.

When to go to hospital

Go to a hospital emergency department if you have:

  • Blood or black colour in your stool
  • A high fever, or fever with chills
  • Severe or worsening stomach pain
  • Vomiting that stops you keeping any fluids down
  • Very little or no urine for many hours
  • Fainting, confusion or extreme weakness

Take extra care with babies and young children, pregnant women, older people and anyone with diabetes, kidney, heart or bowel disease, or a weakened immune system. Call a doctor early for them. Fever in Bali can also be dengue, so avoid aspirin and ibuprofen until a doctor has checked you.

Treatment options

  1. At home, for mild cases: oral rehydration solution, rest and simple food. Many cases need nothing more.
  2. A doctor's visit when symptoms are strong, last more than a day or two, or you are worried. The doctor checks how dehydrated you are and whether you need medicine. Do not take antibiotics or anti-diarrhoea tablets without medical advice: antibiotics are only needed for some cases.
  3. IV rehydration when you cannot keep fluids down or are clearly dehydrated. Fluids and medicine go straight into the blood through a drip, which helps when vomiting makes drinking impossible.

Can you claim Bali belly on travel insurance?

Many travel insurance policies cover doctor visits and treatment for illness abroad, including at your villa or hotel. Whether yours does depends on your policy, so check the wording or call your insurer.

Insurers usually ask for:

  • A medical report in English with the diagnosis and treatment
  • An itemized receipt showing what was paid
  • Your policy number and travel dates

How to avoid Bali belly

  • Drink bottled or filtered water, and use it to brush your teeth
  • Wash or sanitise your hands before eating
  • Choose food that is cooked and served hot
  • Be careful with raw salads, unpeeled fruit and ice from unknown places
  • Keep a few packets of oralit in your villa, just in case

Bali belly: common questions

What is Bali belly?

Bali belly is the name travellers use for travellers' diarrhoea in Bali: a stomach infection, usually from bacteria in food or water, that causes diarrhoea, stomach cramps and sometimes nausea, vomiting or a mild fever.

How long does Bali belly last?

Most cases improve within 3 to 5 days with fluids and rest. See a doctor if it lasts more than 3 days, gets worse, or comes with a high fever, blood in the stool or vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down.

What should I eat and drink with Bali belly?

Sip oral rehydration solution (oralit) or bottled water often in small amounts. When you feel ready, start with simple foods such as plain rice, bananas, toast, crackers and clear soup, and avoid alcohol, coffee, dairy, spicy and greasy food until you recover.

When should I go to hospital with Bali belly?

Go to a hospital emergency department if you have blood in your stool, a high fever, severe or worsening stomach pain, you cannot keep any fluids down, you pass very little urine, or you feel faint, confused or very weak.
