Surf Rehabilitation & Recovery Guide

Common Surfing Injuries in Bali & How Physiotherapy Helps

By Dripwell Bali Medical Team • Updated: September 2026 • 6 Min Read

Bali is globally renowned for world-class waves, attracting thousands of surfers to iconic breaks in Uluwatu, Canggu, Pererenan, and Keramas every year. However, paddling out for hours, fighting strong currents, and taking heavy wipeouts on shallow reefs can take a severe physical toll on your body.

Whether you are a seasoned surfer pushing through marathon sessions or a beginner adapting to paddling fatigue, muscle strain and joint injuries are common. Understanding the **most frequent surfing injuries in Bali**—and knowing how targeted mobile physiotherapy can accelerate your recovery—is the key to staying in the water throughout your trip.

1. "Surfer's Shoulder" (Rotator Cuff Impingement & Tendonitis)

Paddling accounts for nearly 50% to 60% of your total time in the water. Overusing the shoulder muscles during constant, repetitive stroke motions frequently leads to **rotator cuff strain** or **subacromial impingement**.

  • Symptoms: Dull ache at the top or side of the shoulder, sharp pinch when lifting the arm overhead, and difficulty paddling at full strength.
  • How Physiotherapy Helps: Targeted ultrasound therapy reduces local inflammation, while manual soft-tissue mobilization releases tight subscapularis and infraspinatus muscles.

Physiotherapist's Tip:

Never push through sharp shoulder pain while paddling. Rotator cuff micro-tears can worsen rapidly, leading to prolonged immobility if left untreated without targeted physical therapy.

2. Lower Back Pain & Lumbar Strain

To maintain visibility while paddling, surfers arch their back continuously for extended periods (lumbar hyperextension). Combined with explosive pop-ups, this posture places immense compressive force on the lower back vertebrae and facet joints.

  • Symptoms: Stiffness along the lower spine, muscle tightness in the glutes or hips, and discomfort when bending backward.
  • How Physiotherapy Helps: Using TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) combined with deep tissue lumbar release, a physiotherapist relaxes spinal erectors and restores core mobility.

3. Neck Strains & Whiplash from Wipeouts

Sudden heavy wipeouts or duck-diving under large sets can force the neck into harsh hyper-flexion or extension. Additionally, holding the head up while lying on the board strains the upper trapezius and cervical muscles.

  • Symptoms: Neck stiffness, limited head rotation, tension headaches starting from the base of the skull, and muscle spasms.
  • How Physiotherapy Helps: Infrared heat lamp therapy penetrates deep into cervical muscle tissue, preparing the area for gentle myofascial release and spinal alignment techniques.

4. Ankle Sprains & Knee Ligament Strain

Sharp cutbacks, pumping through flat sections, or landing awkwardly on a board can twist the knee or ankle joints. Reef cuts or board impacts can also cause secondary joint inflammation.

  • Symptoms: Localized swelling, tenderness along the knee ligament (MCL/LCL), and pain during weight-bearing movements or pop-ups.
  • How Physiotherapy Helps: Electrotherapy reduces articular effusion (swelling), followed by structured proprioceptive exercises to rebuild stability for surfing.

Red Flag Symptoms: When to Seek Immediate Medical Evaluation

While soft-tissue strains respond exceptionally well to physiotherapy, certain severe surf injuries require immediate medical evaluation by a doctor:

  • Inability to bear any weight on your leg, knee, or ankle after a wipeout
  • Visible joint dislocation, deformity, or severe, rapid joint swelling
  • Numbness, tingling, or radiating pain shooting down your arm or leg
  • Deep reef cuts showing signs of infection (redness, warmth, pus, fever)
  • Severe head impact accompanied by dizziness, nausea, or loss of consciousness
