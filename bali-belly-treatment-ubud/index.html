Questions About Bali Belly Treatment in Ubud

How fast can you reach me in Ubud?

Our team usually arrives within 60–90 minutes after your booking is confirmed, depending on traffic and your exact address.

Will I need antibiotics?

Not always. Many cases of Bali Belly improve with rehydration and rest. The doctor assesses your symptoms and decides whether medication such as an antibiotic is needed.

When should I go to hospital instead?

Go to a hospital emergency department if you have blood in your stool, a high fever that does not come down, severe abdominal pain, confusion, fainting, or you cannot keep any fluids down for many hours.

Are Dripwell Bali packages IV drip packages?

Yes. Every Dripwell Bali package is an IV drip (infusion) treatment given at your villa, hotel or residence. It includes the IV medications and recovery vitamins listed in your package, a doctor and a nurse, transport and follow-up. The doctor assesses you first, and the treatment is given only if it is suitable for you.

Who comes to my villa or hotel?

A doctor and a nurse come to every treatment. The doctor assesses your symptoms and medical history first, and the treatment is given only if it is suitable for you.

Is transport included in the price?

Yes. Transport to your accommodation is included in the package price, so there is no separate call-out fee.

What happens after the treatment?

Our team follows up on your recovery after the treatment, and you can contact the doctor directly if you have any concerns. Dripwell Bali does not provide wound care; for wounds or injuries please go to a clinic or hospital.

Are lab tests included in the package?

No. Lab tests are only arranged when the doctor finds them necessary, and they are charged separately from the package. Ask the team about the cost before the test is done.

What should I do if I have severe symptoms?

Do not wait for a mobile visit if you have severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, fainting, confusion, difficulty breathing, chest pain or signs of severe dehydration. Go to the nearest hospital emergency department.
